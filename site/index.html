24h

Since 11 September, an exploited dependency comes with a 24-hour deadline.

If you sell software or connected hardware in the EU, the Cyber Resilience Act now requires an early warning within 24 hours of becoming aware of an actively exploited vulnerability in your product. Products already on the market are included. So is the dependency three levels down: it ships inside your product.

  1. 11 Sep 2026 Reporting obligations apply
  2. 11 Dec 2027 Full obligations, including CE marking

Vulnpilot shows which of your dependencies are on the CISA list today, and its three-question screen tells you whether the CRA likely applies. Run the CRA screen

Fails CI only on what is exploited

With --fail-on kev, a new medium advisory does not block a release. A dependency in the KEV catalog does. The daily run catches new catalog entries between commits.

How is this different from npm audit or Dependabot?

They tell you what is vulnerable. Vulnpilot tells you which of those are known to be exploited, using the CISA KEV catalog, puts them first, ranks the rest by EPSS and CVSS, and screens whether the CRA applies. Run it next to them: it reads the same lockfile.

What leaves my machine?

Package names and versions, sent to OSV.dev, and CVE IDs, sent to FIRST for EPSS scores. The CISA catalog is downloaded; nothing is sent to CISA. Source code, file paths and manifests stay local. The CLI’s anonymous telemetry sends five coarse fields per scan, is off in CI, and --no-telemetry turns it off. In the browser scanner, nothing leaves the tab until you press Scan, and the page lists what will be sent first.

Is this legal advice?

No. The CRA screen is a rule-based first pass from three answers: EU market, commercial activity and product type. It returns likely applies, likely out of scope or unclear, with the reason and the key dates. Ask a qualified adviser before you rely on it.

Our product is in Python, Go or Java. Does it work?

Yes, through a CycloneDX JSON SBOM. Generate one with your build’s CycloneDX tooling and pass it with --sbom. Supported purl types include pypi, golang and maven.

Which ecosystems are supported?

Lockfiles: package-lock.json (v1–3), pnpm-lock.yaml (5.x, 6.x, 9.x) and yarn.lock (Classic and Berry). Anything else through a CycloneDX JSON SBOM with --sbom, for purl types npm, pypi, maven, golang, cargo, gem, nuget, composer, hex, pub, conan, deb and apk.

Why is the scanner free?

A scanner you can read is easier to trust. The code is MIT-licensed on GitHub and the CLI stays free. We plan to charge for monitoring and early-warning drafts, not for the scan.

What will the paid product cost?

Not decided yet. We’ll set scope and pricing with the design partners.

Does it work offline or in CI?

Both. Responses are cached for 24 hours and --offline uses the cache only. In CI, pass --non-interactive with the three answers as flags, read the versioned --json report, and gate on exit codes with --fail-on critical|high|kev.
